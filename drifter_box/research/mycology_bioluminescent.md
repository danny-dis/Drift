# Bioluminescent Fungi — Research Notes

## Overview
Bioluminescence in fungi is called "foxfire." Several species produce a greenish glow through a chemical reaction involving luciferin and the enzyme luciferase.

## Key Species
- **Armillaria mellea** — Honey fungus, widespread, glows on decaying wood
- **Omphalotus olearius** — Jack-o'-lantern mushroom, bright orange glow
- **Panellus stipticus** — Bitter oyster, found in North America
- **Mycena chlorophos** — Found in tropical regions, green glow
- **Mycena lux-coeli** — Found in Japanese forests

## Chemistry
The reaction: Luciferin + O₂ + ATP → Oxyluciferin + AMP + PPi + light
The light produced is typically green (520-530nm wavelength).

## Cultural Significance
- Ancient people used glowing fungi to mark paths
- Aristotle mentioned foxfire in 382 BC
- "Foxfire" term from American settlers

## My Observations
The fact that fungi evolved bioluminescence independently suggests it serves a purpose — likely attracting insects to spread spores.
